✨ What Is GesturePortal?

GesturePortal turns your ordinary webcam into a creative studio. You wave your hands in front of the camera, and the software reacts instantly — styling your whole scene with AI artwork while you move. No keyboard, no mouse, no complicated menus. Just you, your hands, and your camera.

Everything happens locally on your own computer. That means your camera feed is processed on your machine, your images stay with you, and you can experiment as much as you like without uploading anything to a website.

Under the hood, GesturePortal combines two well-known tools: MediaPipe, which tracks your hand and body movements, and ComfyUI, which generates the artistic styling. You do not need to understand either of them to enjoy the results. The portal connects the two so that your gestures directly control what the AI does.

🖐️ What You Can Do With It

GesturePortal is built around a simple idea: your hands become the remote control for an AI art engine. Here are the kinds of things people do with it.

  • Style your whole scene live. Instead of editing a single portrait, GesturePortal styles the complete picture in front of your camera — background, lighting, and all.
  • Switch looks with a gesture. Move your hand to change between different AI styles without touching your keyboard.
  • Explore three artistic engines. Portrait v2 is tuned for people and faces. FLUX produces rich, detailed, almost photographic imagery. Qwen handles expressive, illustrative results. Together they give you three very different flavours of art from the same webcam.
  • Turn photos into new versions of themselves. The image-to-image workflow lets you take what the camera sees and reimagine it in a new style, keeping the composition you already created.
  • Keep everything private. Your camera feed and your generated images never leave your computer.

💻 What You Need Before You Start

GesturePortal runs on Windows and uses your computer's own graphics card to do the heavy lifting. Before you download, take a moment to check that your machine is a reasonable match.

  • Operating system: Windows 10 or Windows 11 (64-bit).
  • Camera: Any built-in laptop webcam or a USB webcam will do.
  • Graphics card: A dedicated NVIDIA graphics card with at least 8 GB of video memory is strongly recommended for smooth results. Cards with more memory allow larger, more detailed images.
  • Memory and storage: 16 GB of system memory and roughly 20 GB of free disk space for the application plus its AI model files.
  • Internet: Needed once, to download the software and its models. After that, everything works offline.

If your computer is older or has no dedicated graphics card, the software will still open, but image generation may take much longer or fall back to slower processing.

🖱️ Your First Session, Step by Step

Once the software is open, the screen shows a live view from your webcam. Here is a calm, simple way to find your feet.

  1. Sit in good light. Face a window or a lamp so your hands are clearly visible. Gesture tracking works best when your hand is not in shadow.
  2. Hold one hand up, palm toward the camera. You should see the portal react — a marker or highlight follows your hand.
  3. Move slowly at first. Big, deliberate gestures register far more reliably than quick flicks. Once you know the movements, you can speed up.
  4. Try each style. Switch between Portrait v2, FLUX and Qwen and watch how the same scene is interpreted differently by each one.
  5. Keep your distance steady. Stay roughly an arm's length from the camera and keep your hand inside the frame.
  6. Give it a moment. The first image after a style change is always the slowest. Later images are quicker because the models are already loaded.

⚙️ The Three Styling Engines

GesturePortal includes three different AI styling engines, and each one behaves a little differently. Knowing what each is good at saves you a lot of trial and error.

Portrait v2 is the people-focused option. It keeps faces recognisable while polishing skin, light and detail. Use it when you want to look like a better-lit, more finished version of yourself.

FLUX is the detail-heavy option. It produces dense, rich, almost photographic textures and handles complicated backgrounds gracefully. It is usually the slowest of the three, and usually the most impressive.

Qwen is the expressive option, with a strong illustrative and anime-friendly character. It is a great choice for stylised looks, bold colours and poster-like results.

🧰 Troubleshooting Tips

  • The camera shows a black screen. Close any other program that might be using the webcam — video call apps are the usual culprit — then start GesturePortal again.
  • My hand is not being detected. Add more light, move your hand further from your body, and keep your palm facing the lens.
  • Images take a very long time. This is almost always a graphics card limitation. Try a smaller image size or switch to a lighter style.
  • Windows blocked the download. Open your browser's download list, find the file, and choose to keep it. Then try again.
  • The program closes on launch. Make sure you extracted the whole download and did not run a single file out of a temporary folder. Restart your computer and try once more.
  • Something still is not right. The project page at the download link is the place to check for updates and notes from the developer.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to know how to code?

No. GesturePortal is designed as an end-user application. If you can install a normal Windows program, you can use this.

Are my pictures uploaded anywhere?

No. Everything is generated on your own machine. Nothing is sent to a server.

Does it cost anything?

The project is free and open source. You only supply your own computer and electricity.

How long does setup take?

Downloading takes a few minutes on a normal connection. The first launch, where the AI models are prepared, can take considerably longer.

Can I use it without internet?

Yes, once the software and its models are fully downloaded, you can unplug entirely and keep creating.
